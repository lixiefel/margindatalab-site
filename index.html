01 — The premise

Most cafés set prices by feel, copy the shop next door, and never revisit it. The margin you're missing is already on the menu.

A few cents in the wrong place adds up over thousands of cups. The fix isn't raising everything — it's knowing which item can take a lift and which must stay cheap.

02 — The engine

Not a guess. A model — and a test to check it.

Eight passes turn your menu, costs and a month of sales into a defensible price for every item — at the counter and on the apps.

i. Calibrated demand

How sensitive is each item to price?

A demand model tuned to reproduce your actual sales, item by item. Price sensitivity starts from published research until a short price test measures it.

ε = elasticity  →  the band, until a test measures it
Demand curve · "Latte"ε = −1.50 · plausible band
ii. Lerner-optimal markup

The price that maximises profit, exactly.

The Lerner rule gives one item's profit-maximising markup. The engine solves every price together, because raising one item moves guests to its neighbours.

P* = c · |ε| / (|ε| − 1)
Profit vs price · "Latte"peak → €4.20
iii. Stress-tested moves

We move as far as the data earns.

Every move is re-run with price sensitivity from ×0.3 to ×2. A move that only pays under one assumption is cut back or held.

How much of the move survivesrobust / partly / held
iv. Role-aware guardrails

An anchor and a magnet aren't priced the same way.

Each item has a job. A Traffic Driver stays cheap on purpose; a Premium Anchor can stretch. Every move is capped to the item's role.

Allowed price movement by roleguardrail bands
v. Psychology & charm endings

The same money, in a number that feels right.

€4.00 and €3.95 earn nearly the same but read differently. We round to endings that feel right and stay clear of barrier prices — in euros or rupiah.

Raw optimum → charmed pricebarrier-aware
vi. Menu-engineering quadrants

Every item, placed on the map.

Popularity against profit sorts the menu: Stars to protect, Plowhorses to firm up, Puzzles to reposition, Dogs to fix or cut.

Hover any point.

vii. Sensitivity analysis

Which assumption actually moves the answer?

We shake every key input — price sensitivity, new-customer share, costs, commissions — to see which ones move the answer.

Then we measure the biggest: two prices alternating on GoFood every three days for six to twelve weeks, reversible in seconds.

Optimal, but never out of step.

Your current and recommended prices, plotted against the cafés around you — so every move is profit-optimal and defensible to a regular.

05 — Honest by design

Three things a model can't see.

Each of these needs someone who knows your café. That's the conversation.

L·01

How strongly your guests react to price

The model knows your sales, costs and fees — not how hard your guests push back on a price rise. Research fills in until we test it.

So we talk: which item to test first.
L·02

The basket & attach rate

A coffee often brings a pastry. Push the coffee and you may lose the pastry.

So we talk: where holding a price protects the basket.
L·03

Day-part & weekday mix

A morning rush and a quiet Tuesday behave differently. The model averages the month.

So we talk: when your margin is really made.

That's why the audit comes with a conversation, not just a PDF.
