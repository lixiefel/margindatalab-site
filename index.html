01 — The premise

Most cafés set prices by feel, copy the shop next door, and never revisit it. Where margin is missing, it is often already on the menu.

A few cents in the wrong place adds up over thousands of cups. The fix isn't raising everything — it's knowing which item can take a lift and which must stay cheap.

Priced by intuition

Gut feel, and a glance at the café next door.

Frozen for years

Costs rise; prices don't. Margin leaks quietly.

Left on the table

Prices that no longer cover rising costs. We look for them.

02 — The method

Evidence first, small steps, then measure.

How we work, in words. This is an illustration of the method, not a result for any café.

i

Your full costs

Recipe cost, packaging, waste and app fees, item by item. An item that earns less than its full cost is flagged first.

ii

What each delivery order leaves you

The app's cut, checked against your own settlement statement where you have one.

iii

The market around you

Regular prices at comparable cafés nearby, as a reference point. Promo prices are left out.

iv

A model to choose what to test

A demand model, starting from published research, points to the items worth testing. On its own it does not set your prices.

v

Small steps you approve

An app price below its floor (what an order needs to cover its costs) is raised to that floor. Other changes are small steps from today's price. Nothing changes without your approval.

vi

Measure where sales allow

If you sell enough, a price test checks a move before you keep it. If you don't, we say so plainly.

◦ Illustration only

We don't promise a profit increase. How your guests react to a new price only shows after the change, and at small volumes that takes a long time to see. We tell you how sure we are, item by item.

03 — Honest by design

Three things a model can't see.

Each of these needs someone who knows your café. That's the conversation.

L·01

How strongly your guests react to price

The model knows your sales, costs and fees — not how hard your guests push back on a price rise. Research fills in until we test it.

So we talk: which item to test first.
L·02

The basket & attach rate

A coffee often brings a pastry. Push the coffee and you may lose the pastry.

So we talk: where holding a price protects the basket.
L·03

Day-part & weekday mix

A morning rush and a quiet Tuesday behave differently. The model averages the month.

So we talk: when your margin is really made.

That's why the audit comes with a conversation, not just a PDF.
